# Code of conduct

Skipper is a small project that is meant to be built in the open. This page says how people are expected to treat each other while they do it. It is short on purpose.

**Status as of 0.1.0-test.6 (2026-11-09):** Skipper is not released and contributions are not open yet (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) and the [README](README.md)). This code applies from the start, to everyone who takes part, including the maintainers and the owner.

## What we expect

- **Be fair and assume good faith.** Treat others as you would want to be treated in a shared workshop. Assume the other person means well until they show otherwise.
- **Talk about the work, not the person.** Criticise a design, a text or a change as firmly as it needs. Never criticise the person who wrote it.
- **Give reasons.** Say what is wrong and why, and point to the section or line. A bare "this is bad" helps nobody.
- **Accept decisions.** You may disagree, and you should say so once, with reasons. After a decision is made, raise it again only if you have something new. The design marks who decides what (see rule 6 in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)).
- **Be patient with language and experience.** Everything here is written in English, and people write it at very different levels. Plain words are welcome. Mocking someone's English or their experience is not.
- **Respect people's time.** Skipper has one maintainer (see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)), and replies are best effort. Do not demand quick answers and do not ping people over and over.
- **Protect privacy.** Never post anyone's personal data, credentials, tokens or session transcripts, your own included. See rule 8 in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **Be honest about origins.** If something is not entirely your own work, say so. Do not submit what you have no right to share.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, name-calling, mockery and personal attacks.
- Harassment: repeated unwanted contact, following someone from one place to another, intimidation and threats.
- Hate or discrimination, for example against someone's age, disability, gender, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, religion, appearance or skill level.
- Sexual language or images aimed at others, and unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing private information about others, such as addresses, private messages or workplaces, without their consent.
- Spam: advertising, off-topic posts, and mass-produced or automated posts that no person has read before sending.
- Pressure and bad faith: reopening a settled question again and again, or trying to wear people down.
- Knowingly submitting harmful, copied or private material, or trying to slip something past a review.
- Punishing someone for making a report or for helping to handle one.
- Helping or encouraging any of the above.

Strong disagreement about a technical point is not a conduct problem as long as it stays polite and on the subject. A blunt review comment about the work is fine.

## Scope

This code applies in every place where the project works or speaks: issues, pull requests, reviews, comments, discussions, commit messages, documents, the project's Discord server, and any channel the project opens later. It also applies outside those places when someone is clearly speaking for Skipper, or when behaviour aimed at another participant makes it unreasonable for that person to take part.

## Reporting a problem

Report privately. Apart from the contentless contact request below, never report a conduct problem in a public issue, pull request, comment, discussion or Discord channel. A public post can expose the people involved and make things worse.

Contact: the project owner, [@Nissuoh](https://github.com/Nissuoh), through GitHub's private reporting. Open the repository's Security tab, choose "Report a vulnerability" (<https://github.com/Nissuoh/skipper/security/advisories/new>), and start the title with "Conduct:". Only the maintainers and you can see such a report. If the button is missing, open a public issue with the "Design feedback" form, because blank issues are off. Set the title to "Design: conduct contact needed", type "conduct contact needed" in "Where" and in "What did you notice?", choose "A question", and tick both boxes (this request is only a contact request and holds no details). Add nothing else.

You can also message a Moderator on the Discord server: send a direct message to a member with the Moderator or Maintainer role. If the report concerns a Moderator or the project owner, use Discord's own report function instead. This is an extra route, and the one above stays. A Moderator never messages anyone to ask for a password, a sign-in code or a token.

No e-mail address is published for reports.

A useful report says what happened, where (a link if there is one), when, and who was involved. Include only as much private detail as is needed to understand it.

What happens next:

- A maintainer reads the report and keeps it confidential. It is shared only with people who need to know in order to act.
- Replies are best effort, and there is no fixed deadline. You will be told the outcome where that is possible.
- Anyone involved in the matter takes no part in deciding it. If the report is about a maintainer, that maintainer is left out. With one maintainer, a report about the maintainer cannot be handled inside the project. In that case use GitHub's own route for reporting abuse instead: [Reporting abuse or spam](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
- A security vulnerability is not a conduct problem. Report those as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## What maintainers may do

Maintainers decide how to respond. The response should fit the problem: a quiet word for a small slip, firmer steps for serious or repeated behaviour. There is no fixed scale, and nothing here promises a warning first. Maintainers may:

- ask someone to change or withdraw a comment;
- edit, hide, delete or lock comments, issues, pull requests and discussions that break this code or are off-topic or spam, and say why where that helps;
- warn someone in private;
- decline or close contributions from someone;
- block someone from the project's spaces, for a while or for good;
- report abuse to the hosting platform.

If you think a decision was wrong, you can ask for a second look through the same contact. Maintainers are bound by this code like everyone else.
